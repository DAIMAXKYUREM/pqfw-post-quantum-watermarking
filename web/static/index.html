PQFW — post-quantum forensic watermarking

One document, encrypted once for every recipient. Each recipient's decryption produces a visually identical but uniquely fingerprinted copy — and the fingerprint is enforced by which keys they hold, not by software that politely applies a watermark. A leaked copy traces back to a recipient with a stated false-accusation probability, corroborated by an append-only ledger.

1 Enrol recipients

Each recipient gets an ML-KEM-768 key pair (to receive their key bundle) and an ML-DSA-65 key pair (to sign a receipt when they decrypt). Three independent witnesses are created for ledger checkpoints, two of which must sign for a checkpoint to count.

2 Protect the document

The document is split into a base body plus m mark slots. Each slot is rendered two ways that look identical, and the two renderings are encrypted under different keys. Both ship to everybody — the ciphertext below is byte-identical for every recipient. Only a ~14 KB wrapped key bundle differs, and it decides which rendering of each slot that person can decrypt.

3 Decrypt as one recipient

This runs the recipient's side: ML-KEM decapsulation, unwrap the bundle, decrypt one variant per slot, then sign an ML-DSA-65 receipt that is appended to the hash-chained ledger. The copy they get carries a fingerprint they could not have declined.

4 Trace a leak

Paste or upload a leaked copy. The slots are read back, scored against every issued codeword, and the result is reported with a provable upper bound on the probability that an innocent recipient would score this highly — plus the ledger evidence. A trace is only called conclusive when all five checks hold.

5 Audit the ledger

Every decryption is a signed entry in a hash chain, checkpointed by a Merkle root that two of three independent witnesses sign. The ledger stores only a salted commitment to each codeword, never the codeword — so it could be published in full without giving anyone the means to forge or strip a mark. Corrupt an entry and watch what breaks.

Real cryptography, running server-side: liboqs ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65, AES-256-GCM, SHA3-256, and a symmetric Tardos fingerprinting code. Nothing on this page is pre-computed or simulated.

Known limits, stated deliberately. The text carrier does not survive whitespace normalisation, retyping, OCR, or having a word inserted — the declared threat model is redistribution of the file, a screenshot or a print, not manual reconstruction. The distributor knows every codeword, so the guarantee is traceability, not unframeability; asymmetric fingerprinting needs a post-quantum Paillier substitute that does not yet exist. Every one of these is measured in the project's evaluation harness, including the attacks that win.
